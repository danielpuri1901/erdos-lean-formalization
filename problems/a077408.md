## Plain statement

Start with 103, write it in base 3, flip the digit string backwards, add the flipped number to the original, and repeat; the claim is that the result never reads the same forwards and backwards.

## Definitions

rev3(n): write n in base 3 (digits 0,1,2, no leading zeros), reverse the digit order, read it back as a base-3 number (trailing zeros of n vanish). Palindrome: n = rev3(n). a(0)=103, a(k+1)=a(k)+rev3(a(k)). Claim: a(k) is never a palindrome. First 15 terms a(0..14) in base 10: 103, 230, 436, 776, 2424, 3856, 7400, 20856, 30928, 60920, 220248, 242704, 432896, 857152, 1460408.

## Checked so far (Python, 2026-10-04, not a proof)

Python, exact big integers: trajectory of 103 for 6000 steps (final term has 3583 base-3 digits), no palindrome at any step. All starts 1..102 reach a palindrome in at most 18 steps (slowest 53 and 79), so 103 is the smallest start that does not within the 2000-step cap. No failure.

## Attack ideas

Look for a repeating block pattern in the digit strings (the way binary Lychrel cases are proved) and prove by induction that every term keeps a non-palindromic frame; or push the trajectory far past 10^5 steps looking for a palindrome.

## Risks and notes

No proof technique known for this base; the digit pattern may or may not be regular. Lean statement checked against the docstring: revBase via digit reversal, matches.
